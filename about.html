about

A short version

I'm Hüseyn Teymurzade. I study computer engineering at Marmara University and spend most of my time trying to understand what actually happens in the lower layers of a computer.

My interest in software started with wondering how computers work. That curiosity led me first to C and Python, and then to the part I genuinely enjoy: managing memory by hand, building an emulator's instruction loop, watching an interpreter parse its own language.

These days I mostly write Rust, Zig, Go, C and Odin. Most of what I build lives in the terminal — system monitors, file analysis tools, games, interpreters. My daily environment is Linux, usually NixOS; I like keeping everything reproducible.

My goal isn't only to become a good engineer, but to leave behind software that is useful, open, and readable enough for someone else to learn from. I treat every new project as an excuse to learn something — and it usually works out that way.

Computer science is no more about computers than astronomy is about telescopes.
— Edsger W. Dijkstra
